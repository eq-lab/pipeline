# Why commodity trade finance

Commodity trade finance is short-duration secured lending against physical goods in transit or in storage. A trader buys a cargo from a seller and sells it to an end-buyer. It needs working capital between paying the seller and receiving payment from the end-buyer. The loan is secured by the goods and the papers that control them: bills of lading, warehouse receipts, storage agreements. It repays from the sale proceeds. Durations run 30 to 180 days. It is the oldest secured lending product in commerce.

## Structural advantages of the asset class

Five features keep historic default rates below 0.3% across the 2008 financial crisis, COVID-19, and 2022 commodity volatility (ICC Trade Register).

| Property | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Self-liquidating** | The loan repays from sale proceeds from a pre-contracted offtaker. |
| **Short duration** | 30–180 day duration with rapid capital recycle. |
| **Physical collateral** | Standardised commodities with transparent pricing and deep secondary markets. Physically verifiable, sold in days. |
| **Established legal framework** | Bills of lading and warehouse receipts have centuries of commercial law behind them. UCC Article 7 in the US. |
| **Third-party verification** | Independent collateral managers (SGS, Cotecna, Intertek) inspect cargo. |

## The $800 billion gap

Basel III raised capital requirements, and commodity trade finance became uneconomic for banks. Regulators treat a 60-day secured trade loan and a 5-year unsecured corporate loan similarly for capital purposes. The trade loan generates a fraction of the fee revenue.

The financing gap, the difference between trade finance requested and approved, has reached $800 billion. Mid-market traders pay double-digit rates against sub-0.3% historical default risk.

## Why it has not been on-chain before

Trade finance needs infrastructure other on-chain credit protocols do not have: cargo monitoring, independent market prices for daily valuation, a security agent who holds the goods' papers and can enforce, KYB and KYT of every counterparty, and bank accounts for offtaker wires. Each is operationally heavy. Pipeline puts these together with on-chain capital and a public loan registry.
